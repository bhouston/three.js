# Multi-scale CDF energy (experimental)

`generate({ size, depth, seed, passes: 4, thresholdCount: 31, spatialSigmas: [0.7, 1], temporalAlpha: 0.1, temporalWeight: 1, initialVolume, onProgress })` returns a Float32 volume. `initialVolume` is copied, never mutated. Every accepted operation exchanges two values, preserving its exact global histogram. Generation and refinement are deterministic for fixed options. Without a warm start it starts with one jittered sample per temporal stratum per pixel.

This extends the cached-threshold optimizer with Gaussian spatial autocorrelation mixtures, and an exact periodic EMA autocorrelation instead of a short truncated EMA. The 31 training thresholds are midpoint CDF samples; held-out thresholds and nonlinear functions still need independent evaluation. The energy sums spatial and temporal error separately, matching the broad FAST separate design. It does not claim domination or novel state of the art. Scale mixing deliberately includes a narrower filter to reduce over-specialization to sigma=1. It does not optimize the spatial/temporal product filter.

The cache stores one Float64 potential per voxel per threshold (about 130 MB at 128x128x32 and 31 thresholds). Candidate swaps only touch crossed thresholds. Kernel aliases are consolidated, including on tiny volumes. `onProgress` reports cumulative accepted swaps and the exact predicted unnormalized energy change (negative or zero); it is called at each pass and at completion. Normalizations divide each spatial filter's squared error by its self autocorrelation, and the EMA squared error by its impulse squared norm; thresholds have equal weight.

Validation: independent direct spatial Gaussian and cyclic EMA filtering at 4x4x4, 7 thresholds, warm start: energy decreased 2080.4870814627734 to 2055.2627482601624 after four passes. Bitwise repeatability and exact histogram preservation passed. A 32x32x8 scratch smoke run with 31 thresholds and four passes completed in 0.059 seconds and accepted 4,082 swaps; this is a small-size smoke timing, not a representative performance comparison. Full-size timing is intentionally left to the coordinated benchmark. No GPU is involved.

Tradeoffs: finite CDF features can miss unseen functions; lowering their aggregate energy does not guarantee improving every individual metric. Greedy refinement of a FAST volume can sacrifice one property for another. Depth-periodic EMA corresponds to steady periodic operation, and differs from an EMA initialized at zero. Quality must be evaluated over temporal phases, independent seeds, threshold holdouts and other functions. Broader kernels increase initialization and accepted-swap update costs.
